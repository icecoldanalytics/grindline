Where to bet in Alberta's regulated market

As of July 13, 2026, Alberta's open iGaming market means real, licensed sportsbooks are legally available in-province. Here's a practical rundown for NHL bettors — specifically, which books carry the market types our signals and prop models are built to analyze.

This never affects our signals, models, or tracked results — we never take bets, and our analytics are graded publicly whether they win or lose. Only bet with operators licensed in your province, and only bet what you can afford to lose.

What matters for our signals

Our moneyline signal — away team on a back-to-back with the home team rested exactly two days — is a standard h2h play that every licensed book carries. Where books differ is player prop depth: we publish a shots-on-goal and points prop model as research, graded against real prices. We tested it across 12,576 priced props and it did not beat the market, so we do not sell it as picks — but a book with a deep, early prop board is useful for following the research and tracking where the market sits relative to our numbers.

The more meaningful practical difference is moneyline price. Our four-season record is graded at the average US price available at the 7 AM MST snapshot, and across 508 games we measured that best-price shopping added roughly 1.3 ROI points over taking the average. On a signal that already clears breakeven by about six points, that margin is real — taking −104 instead of −112 on the same home-team bet is the difference between a tighter number and a comfortable one. Having accounts at two or three books and checking before you place is where that 1.3 points is either kept or given back.
