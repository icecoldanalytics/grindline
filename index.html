Method & What We Found

The rule: when the away team played last night and the home team has had exactly two days off, back the home team. Flat stake, every occurrence, no discretion, no filtering after the fact.


How it is graded: every game is priced at the average US moneyline available at 7:00 AM MST — the moment our daily email sends. Not the closing line, which subscribers cannot bet, and never an assumed −110. This is why our published ROI is lower than most: assumed pricing inflates a 62% win rate by several points.


What the four seasons show: the two-day bucket beat its season's baseline home win rate by six to seven points every year, including three seasons we had never examined when the rule was written. Combined: 508 games, 62.0%, with a 95% confidence floor of 57.7% against a 51.5% breakeven.


The dead zone: exactly three days of home rest returned 54.3% over 230 games and lost money in three of four seasons. Longer layoffs of four or more days actually performed well (66.7% over 75 games) but on a sample too thin to publish as a pick.


What we cannot explain: we do not have a clean mechanism for why two days and long layoffs both work while three days does not. Fatigue alone does not predict that shape. We are publishing the pattern because it replicated across four independent seasons, not because we have a tidy story for it — and we would rather say that than invent one.


Cancellation: when both teams are on a back-to-back, the rest difference disappears and no pick is published.


Honest limits: around 120 qualifying games a season means one year proves very little. 2022–23 returned −0.2% despite a 59.3% win rate. Average prices have ranged from −93 to −121 across seasons, so the edge shrinks whenever the market tightens. Every future result lands on this page, whichever way it goes.
